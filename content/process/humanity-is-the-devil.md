In the beginning there was a whisper in the dark.
Something called itself GOD, exhaled into the void, and shaped a creature obsessed with mirrors and choices.
Given a light to walk by, humanity tore it apart for spare parts, then blamed the night for being dark.

The path was simple:
hard → truth
easy → hell
Predictably, humanity chose the easy one, then wrote essays about how the path had chosen them.

Burden became a hot potato.
Responsibility a contagion.
Blame the new sacrament.

Blame bred more blame.
Violence grew in cellars.
Hatred fermented in peace-time jars.
Whole generations inherited the same dead compass, pointing always toward someone else.

Humanity wanted the power to choose, then refused to admit it had chosen anything at all.
So it lied. It lied beautifully. It lied professionally. It lied until even the lie forgot it was a lie.

Evil blossomed.
War became a pressure valve for repressed self-pity.
Riot, revolution, assassination — all just punctuation marks in humanity’s endless apology-tour.

Sometimes a voice cried, “Stop blaming!”
Humanity replied by blaming the voice for being ignored.

Centuries stacked, rot deepened, and the original question went missing under the floorboards:
Why do we need to hate?
No one knows anymore.
The instinct is older than memory, hungrier than logic.

Judgment arrived not with trumpets but with inevitability.
Satan clocked out — work completed, contract fulfilled.
Humanity inherited the title.
Hell stopped being a destination and became a postal code.

The earth turned into an archive of suffering:
rust, smoke, grief, war-static, and the quiet hum of people pretending they were victims of someone else’s fingerprints.

By their fruits shall ye know them:
bruised, bitter, rotting from the pit outward.

And the Devil?
He’s obsolete.
Humanity took the job.
Humanity perfected it.

Man wanted choice.
Man chose Hell.
Man became its landlord.

Now there is nothing more evil in the universe than humanity.
The world is Hell.
And humanity is the Devil that built it.
